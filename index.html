Can you time the market?

A moving-average strategy tries to dodge crashes by selling when the S&P 500 falls below its recent average, and buying back when it recovers. Test it against simply buying and holding, over any period since 1871.

How it works

At the end of each month, the strategy compares the S&P 500 with its average over the last few months. If the index is above the average, it stays invested for the next month. If it’s below, it sells and holds cash. Each switch pays the trading cost you set.

The catch is the lag. An average of past prices only turns after the market already has, so the strategy often sells after a fall and buys back after the rebound has started. In steady bull markets that costs a lot. In long crashes, like 2000–2002 or 2008, it can save a lot. Try the presets to see both.

Data: monthly average S&P 500 prices from Robert Shiller’s dataset, via the DataHub s-and-p-500 package, January 1871 to September 2026. Returns are price-only (no dividends), which understates buy and hold, especially over long periods. Using monthly averages rather than month-end prices smooths the series a little. This is an educational model, not investment advice.
